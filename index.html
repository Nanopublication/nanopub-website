Nanopublications

Small, signed, citable units of knowledge, published on a decentralized network

What is a nanopublication?​

A nanopublication is a small knowledge graph, such as a single claim, data point, or opinion, that is published together with its provenance and metadata as an independent, citable unit. It can stand on its own or accompany a research article, and it can be about anything: a relation between a gene and a disease, the habitat of a beetle species, or the fact that someone has read a paper.

Nanopublications are written in RDF, so they are formal and machine-interpretable. In addition, every nanopublication:

  • has a verifiable identifier: its URI contains a hash of its content (Trusty URI), so anyone can check that it has not been modified;
  • is signed with its creator's key;
  • is immutable: changes are made by publishing a new version that supersedes the old one, or a retraction;
  • lives on a decentralized network of servers that replicate nanopublications and make them queryable with SPARQL.

Structure​

A nanopublication consists of three parts, each an RDF graph, tied together by a small head graph:

  1. Assertion: the main content, a small, atomic unit of information.
  2. Provenance: how the assertion came to be, for example the scientific method or study it is based on.
  3. Publication info: metadata about the nanopublication as a whole, such as who created it, when, and under which license.

Example​

This is a real nanopublication that links a species of ground beetle to a habitat type, shortened and in TriG syntax (full version):

@prefix this: <https://w3id.org/np/RAlohixCBWPuGLSKA7mmR8k01EQvC_dRWwAU70kvvwKPo> .
@prefix sub: <https://w3id.org/np/RAlohixCBWPuGLSKA7mmR8k01EQvC_dRWwAU70kvvwKPo/> .
@prefix np: <http://www.nanopub.org/nschema#> .
@prefix biolink: <https://w3id.org/biolink/vocab/> .
# ... more prefixes

sub:Head {
  this: a np:Nanopublication ;
    np:hasAssertion sub:assertion ;
    np:hasProvenance sub:provenance ;
    np:hasPublicationInfo sub:pubinfo .
}

sub:assertion {
  sub:association a biolink:OrganismTaxonToEnvironmentAssociation ;
    biolink:subject sub:taxon ;
    biolink:predicate obo:RO_0002303 ;      # has habitat
    biolink:object eunis:23467 .            # arable land and market gardens
  sub:taxon kpxl:hasTaxonName gbif:1036789 .  # Carabus granulatus
}

sub:provenance {
  sub:assertion dct:source <https://www.progress.plantprotection.pl/?node_id=35&lang=pl&ma_id=2725&a=2> ;
    prov:wasAttributedTo orcid:0000-0003-2711-4457 .
}

sub:pubinfo {
  this: dct:created "2026-07-28T17:06:19.425Z"^^xsd:dateTime ;
    dct:creator orcid:0000-0002-4587-3476 ;
    dct:license <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/> .
  sub:sig npx:hasAlgorithm "RSA" ;
    npx:hasPublicKey "MIGfMA0GCSqGSIb3DQEBAQUAA4GNADCBiQKBgQCUDeKX..." ;
    npx:hasSignature "Nr/k02mkLbZG9ZCuCzOU7BSlVQoh/rOn9SGCCTmThSZa..." ;
    npx:hasSignatureTarget this: ;
    npx:signedBy orcid:0000-0002-4587-3476 .
}

The RAlohixCBW... part of the URI is a hash of the whole nanopublication, signature included. Change a single triple and it no longer matches.

Why?​

Because nanopublications can be attributed and cited, researchers get credit for publishing their data in standard, machine-readable formats. This makes data easier to find, access, and reuse (see the FAIR principles), with or without an accompanying research article.

Get started​

  • Follow the quick start to fetch, query, verify, and publish nanopublications from the command line.
  • Read the guidelines and look at more examples.
  • Browse, create, and publish nanopublications in your browser with Nanodash.
  • Use one of the libraries for Java, Python, JavaScript, or Rust.
  • Let an AI coding agent do it for you with the nanopub Agent Skill.
  • Read the paper on the nanopublication ecosystem: A Robust Decentralized Infrastructure for Trust-Aware Open Knowledge Sharing (ISWC 2026).
